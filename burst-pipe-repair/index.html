Stop the water first

  1. Find the main shutoff valve, usually where the supply enters the house near the water meter in the basement, and turn it clockwise until it stops.
  2. Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain what remains in the pipes.
  3. If water is near outlets, appliances or the electrical panel, stay out and shut off the breaker only if you can reach it dry.
  4. Move furniture, rugs and valuables out of the wet area and take photos for your records.
  5. Call us. Tell us where the water is, whether it has stopped and whether the ceiling is sagging.

If you cannot find or turn the main, tell us on the call. Some older valves are seized or buried, and we can talk you through the options while a plumber is on the way. A ceiling that bulges with water should be left alone, because it can fall.

Why pipes burst

Freezing

Water expands as it freezes, and pipes in exterior walls, unheated basements and crawlspaces are the usual victims. The split often shows after the thaw.

Corrosion

Old galvanized and thin copper can fail at a weak point without any cold at all.

Pressure and fittings

A failed joint, a bad connector or a surge can open a leak.

Physical damage

A nail, a renovation mistake or settling can puncture a line.

Cold snaps drive burst pipe calls in Delaware County. Pipes in exterior walls, garages and unheated basements are most at risk. Letting a faucet drip during a hard freeze and keeping cabinet doors open on outside walls help, but they do not replace insulation.

Thawing a frozen pipe

If a pipe is frozen but not burst, open the faucet it feeds and warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer or warm towels, working from the faucet toward the frozen part. Never use an open flame. If you see a crack, bulge or water where the pipe was frozen, shut the main and call, because the ice may be holding back a split that will open as it thaws.

Older homes, older pipes

In Upper Darby homes with galvanized or aging copper supply lines, one burst is sometimes a warning. If several pipes have failed, the repiping page explains when replacing the run beats patching it. If the break is on the line from the street, the water service line page applies.

After the pipe is fixed

  • Dry out wet materials quickly. Standing moisture invites mold within days.
  • Photograph damage before cleanup, and keep receipts.
  • Call your insurer. Sudden bursts are often covered, but gradual leaks are not.
  • Ask us whether other pipes in the same wall or run look suspect.
