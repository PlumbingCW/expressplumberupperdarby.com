What we do

We handle emergency and planned plumbing for homes and small businesses: drains, sewer lines, water heaters, leaks, burst pipes, sump pumps, boilers, fixtures and installs. The full list is on our services page, and each service has its own page that explains the work and what to do while you wait.

We do not claim to do everything. Exterior foundation excavation, mold remediation and large industrial jobs are outside our scope, and we tell you so on the first call and suggest the right specialist.

How we work

  • We answer the emergency line around the clock and help you stop the water while a plumber is on the way.
  • We explain what is wrong in plain language and show you what we found.
  • We give a clear price before work starts. For an emergency, we stabilize first and quote before we continue.
  • We do not promise exact arrival times, because traffic and other calls change them.
  • We tell you when a repair is not worth it, and when it is.

Licensed and insured, and what that means locally

Upper Darby Township Code Chapter 265 requires plumbing contractors to employ a licensed master plumber, and only the master plumber can apply for plumbing permits. Township plumbing licenses expire each March 31. We work to that standard.

Pennsylvania also asks contractors doing at least $5,000 a year in home improvement work to register with the Attorney General. You can check any contractor at hicsearch.attorneygeneral.gov. Registration is not an endorsement, but it is a fair first check, and you should check us too.

Where we work

We are based in Upper Darby, PA 19082, and we serve Drexel Hill 19026, Lansdowne and Yeadon 19050, Clifton Heights 19018, Darby 19023 and Haverford Township 19083. Our service areas page links to a page for each town.

Upper Darby is a township of about 85,681 residents on 7.83 square miles, with Cobbs Creek along its eastern border and the 69th Street Transportation Center in Terminal Square. Housing runs from early twentieth-century twins to later rowhouses and apartment buildings, and that variety is why we ask the age of the house on every call.

Our writing

Our plumbing guides are written by the Upper Darby Express Plumbing team. We use primary sources, such as township code and the Pennsylvania Attorney General, and we say when something varies by property.

What a visit looks like

We start on the phone. We ask what you see, where the water is and whether you can shut it off, and we talk you through the shutoff if you need help. A plumber then arrives in the window we gave you, looks at the problem and explains it before touching anything.

You get a clear price before work starts. If the repair is simple, we do it. If it is bigger, such as a sewer line or a repipe, we explain the options and let you decide. When we finish, we test the repair and tell you what to watch for.

The kinds of homes we know

Upper Darby has twins and rowhouses, single homes in Drexel Hill, apartment buildings and shops along Garrett Road and State Road. Each has its own plumbing habits. Rowhouses share walls and sometimes drain lines. Older single homes may have a mix of pipe materials added over decades. Shops need working floor drains and hot water to open at all.

Knowing those patterns helps us ask better questions on the phone. It does not replace looking at the actual pipe, and we always say what we saw rather than what we assumed.

What we will not do

  • We will not quote an arrival time we cannot keep.
  • We will not recommend a replacement when a repair is sound.
  • We will not hide a problem we found along the way.
  • We will not take a job that is outside our scope without saying so.
