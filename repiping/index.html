Whole-House Repiping in Upper Darby, PA

Repiping replaces the water supply pipes in a house, and it is a planned project when galvanized steel, old copper or failing plastic has started to cause low pressure, discolored water or repeated leaks.

Signs the supply pipes are failing

Low pressure at several fixtures

Galvanized pipe corrodes inside and narrows over decades.

Rusty or brown water

Interior corrosion flakes into the water, especially after it sits.

Repeat pinhole leaks

More than one leak in a short time suggests the whole run is aging.

Noisy or banging pipes

Old, loose or narrowed pipes can rattle and knock.

One leak in a good pipe is a repair. Several leaks in the same type of pipe within a few years is a pattern, and each repair takes a section of old pipe and connects it to more old pipe. That is when a repipe starts to make sense.

Galvanized steel and older pipe materials

Galvanized steel pipe, coated with zinc, was common in homes built in the first half of the twentieth century. The zinc wears, the steel rusts and the pipe fills with scale from inside. Flow drops, and the pipe becomes brittle at threaded joints. Water that looks clear in the morning and rusty after sitting overnight is a classic sign.

Copper lasts much longer but can develop pinholes, especially where the water chemistry or installation was poor. Some plastic pipe systems from certain eras have known failure issues. We identify what is in your house before suggesting anything.

What a repipe involves in an occupied home

  1. 1

    Survey

    We trace the supply lines, check access and decide the route.

  2. 2

    Plan

    You get a written scope, schedule and what will be opened.

  3. 3

    Install

    New supply lines go in with the water off for a limited time each day.

  4. 4

    Restore

    We test, patch access holes as agreed and turn the water back on.

A repipe means access, and access means openings in walls and ceilings. In an older home, we use the basement and attic runs as much as we can, and we tell you where we must open a finished wall. We discuss patching, and what is included before the work starts.

Repiping in older Upper Darby housing

Older twins and rowhouses may have supply pipes of different ages in the same house, because additions and repairs added copper to galvanized runs over the years. Connecting different metals can cause corrosion at the joint. We use proper fittings and explain why.

If you are not sure what your pipes are, ask us to look. For a hidden leak without a full repipe, the leak detection page explains the tools, and for a street-to-house problem the water service line page covers it. If a burst has already happened, go to burst pipe repair.

What to expect after a repipe

New supply lines usually mean stronger and steadier water pressure, cleaner water from the tap and fewer leak calls. They do not change the drains, so a house with slow drains still needs drain cleaning or drain repair. We test every connection under pressure before closing access holes, and we explain what was patched and what was left for a finish contractor.
