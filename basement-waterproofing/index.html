Basement Waterproofing in Upper Darby, PA

A wet basement has several possible causes, and the right fix depends on where the water comes from: the ground, the walls, a leaking pipe or a failed sump pump.

We start by working out the source, and we tell you honestly when the fix is a drainage or plumbing repair and when it falls outside what we do.

Find the source before you fix anything

Groundwater

Water rises through the floor or seeps at the wall and floor joint after heavy rain.

Surface water

Gutters, downspouts and grading send water toward the foundation.

Plumbing leaks

A supply, drain or water heater leak can look like a damp basement.

Condensation

Humid air on cool walls and pipes leaves moisture that is not a leak.

A basement that only gets wet after storms points to groundwater or surface water. A basement that is wet all the time, even in dry weather, points to a plumbing leak. A quick test is to tape a square of plastic wrap to a wall: moisture on the room side means condensation, and moisture behind it means water is coming through.

Plumbing-side solutions

Plumbers handle the water management part of basement waterproofing: interior drains, sump pits, pumps, discharge lines and backup systems. A perimeter drain collects water at the footing or under the slab and sends it to a sump pit, where the sump pump sends it out.

We do not claim to repair structural foundation cracks, and we say so early. If a wall is bowing or a crack is growing, a structural engineer or foundation specialist is the right first call.

Interior systems manage water that gets in. They do not stop water from touching the outside of the wall. Some homeowners also fix gutters and grading, which cost less and often reduce the water the interior system has to handle.

Basements in older Upper Darby housing

Many older homes have stone or block foundations that let in moisture more readily than modern poured concrete. Creeks such as Cobbs Creek and Darby Creek run along the edges of the area, and storms can raise groundwater, but we do not claim that any given street floods. Tell us what you see and when, and we will match the fix to the cause.

Mold and moisture

Persistent dampness can lead to mold. We fix the water source, but we do not do mold remediation. If you see widespread mold, a remediation specialist is the right call after the water problem is solved. For a suspected pipe leak behind a wall, leak detection is the first step.

Why we start with a conversation, not a quote

Two houses with the same wet spot can have completely different causes. One has a downspout dumping against the wall, and the other has a failed pipe behind it. We ask when water appears, how much and after what, before we recommend anything. That conversation often saves a homeowner from paying for a system that would not have fixed the problem.

When the answer is a plumbing one, such as a pit, a pump or a discharge line, we handle it. When it is excavation or structural work, we say so and suggest the right specialist. See sump pump installation and repair for the pump side.
