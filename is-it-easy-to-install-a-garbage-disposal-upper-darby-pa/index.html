Is It Easy to Install a Garbage Disposal in Upper Darby, PA?

The honest answer is that it depends on what is under your sink right now. Swapping one disposal for a similar one is much easier than installing the first one in a kitchen that never had it.

This guide walks through both cases so you can decide whether to do it yourself.

Replacement versus a first-time install

Replace with the same mount typeModerateStuck mounting ring, old putty, tight cabinet
Replace with a different brandModerateMount and wiring may not match
First-time installHarderNeeds power, a switch, drain changes and a dishwasher connection
Double-bowl sink changesHarderDifferent drain layout and trap

Most disposals are heavy, so you will want a helper or a jack to hold the unit while you lock it into the mount. Working upside down in a cabinet is the part people underestimate.

What a replacement involves

  1. Cut power at the breaker and confirm the switch is dead.
  2. Disconnect the dishwasher hose and the drain pipe from the old unit.
  3. Twist the unit off its mounting ring and lower it out.
  4. Remove the old wiring cover, disconnect the wires and attach them to the new unit.
  5. Lock the new unit into the mount, reconnect the drain and dishwasher hose and knock out the dishwasher plug if the new unit needs it.
  6. Run water, check for leaks and test the disposal.

The dishwasher knockout plug is the classic mistake. If you leave it in, the dishwasher cannot drain, and it will back up the first time you run it.

What a first-time install adds

A disposal needs a power source, usually an outlet under the sink on a switch above the counter. If the cabinet has no outlet, an electrician or a plumber with the right qualifications has to add one, and that is not a job for guessing. The drain also changes, because the disposal takes the place of the sink strainer and the trap has to line up with its outlet.

We cover both situations on our garbage disposal repair and installation page. If the cabinet plumbing is old or the trap is out of line, the work overlaps with plumbing installation.

Cost, time and risk compared

Doing the job yourself saves labor, but it carries risk. A leak under a cabinet can ruin the base, and electrical mistakes are dangerous. If you are comfortable with tools, have a helper and the sink layout is simple, a replacement is a reasonable weekend task. If you are adding power or changing pipes, hire it out.

Ask yourself three questions before you start. Do I know where the breaker is and how to test that power is off? Can I lift the unit and hold it in place while locking the mount? Do I know what to do if the drain does not line up? If any answer is no, you are better off with a plumber.

After the unit is installed, run cold water while grinding and keep running it for several seconds afterward. Do not put grease, coffee grounds in large amounts, fibrous scraps or bones into the unit, because those cause most of the clogs we see.

Local rules to check before you start

Upper Darby Township has adopted the Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code, and some residential alterations need permits. Adding a new electrical circuit for a disposal is the type of change to check on with the Department of Licenses and Inspection.

Upper Darby Township Code Chapter 265 says plumbing contractors working in the township must employ a licensed master plumber, and only the master plumber can apply for plumbing permits. Plumbing licenses in the township expire on March 31 each year.

Choosing a disposal

  • Continuous feed units are the common type, and they run while you hold the switch.
  • Larger motors handle more food and jam less, but cost more.
  • Stainless steel grinding parts last longer than plated ones.
  • Noise insulation matters if the kitchen is open to a living room.

Things that go wrong after installation

A leak at the flange means the mounting was not sealed properly. A leak at the drain pipe means a loose gasket. A hum with no spin means the unit is jammed, which is covered in the repair section of our service page. A sink that backs up has a drain problem below the disposal, and drain cleaning fixes it.

When to hire help

Hire help if you need new wiring, if the drain has to be rebuilt, if the sink is stone and cannot be drilled safely or if you do not have a helper to lift the unit. A leak under a cabinet can do more damage than the money saved by doing it yourself.

Sources: follow the installation guidelines in your disposal's manual, and note that the International Plumbing Code (IPC) governs the drain and trap connections.

Questions Upper Darby homeowners ask

An outlet or a hardwired connection has to be added, and that work should be done by a qualified electrician or plumber with the right license, with permits where required.

A replacement may take an hour or two. A first-time install takes longer because of the wiring and drain changes.

A homeowner may do their own work. A contractor hired to do plumbing in the township must employ a licensed master plumber under township code.
