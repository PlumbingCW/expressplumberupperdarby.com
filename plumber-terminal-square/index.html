What Terminal Square is

Terminal Square is the area around the 69th Street Transportation Center, home of the Tower Theater at 69th and Ludlow Streets. Delaware County's Downtown Upper Darby Vision Plan describes the area as a mix of commercial and residential uses, with 69th Street as the main corridor and Market Street and Garrett Road as other primary commercial streets. West Chester Pike meets it at the eastern end.

The plan reports that existing land use is mostly commercial and commercial mixed-use, meaning two to four story buildings with apartments over shops. It says downtown buildings were largely built in the 1920s and 1930s, and many nearby residential structures also date from the 1920s. Three to five story apartment buildings are also present.

Why mixed-use buildings need a different plumbing approach

In a building with a shop below and apartments above, drains, vents and water lines are shared. A kitchen drain in a restaurant, a floor drain in a stockroom and a bathroom stack for the apartments may meet in the same basement. A leak or backup in one place can affect another tenant.

We do not say these buildings have a particular type of pipe. We say they are shared systems, so we map who is affected, find the shutoffs and look for the real source before we repair.

Before we arrive

  • Find the main shutoffIt is usually where the water line enters the house near the meter. Turn it clockwise to stop a leak.
  • Open the lowest faucetThis drains the lines and lowers pressure while you wait.
  • Stay clear of wet electricsKeep away from outlets and the panel if the floor is wet.
  • Protect what you canLift rugs and boxes off the floor and take photos of any damage.
  • Have your address readyGive the street, the nearest cross street and the floor or unit if you are in Terminal Square.

In a shared building, tell the property manager. They may know where the main shutoff is and who else is affected.
