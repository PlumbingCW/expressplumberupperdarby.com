When a toilet is an emergency

A toilet that overflows, rocks and leaks at the base or has water coming through the ceiling below needs a plumber now. A toilet that runs or refills by itself is a smaller problem but a wasteful one, and it is one of the most common causes of a high water bill.

We ask what you see: water on the floor, water in the bowl, a rocking base or a hissing tank. Those details tell us whether the fix is a quick part swap, a wax ring and flange repair or a full replacement.

What goes wrong inside the tank

Flapper not sealing

Water leaks from the tank into the bowl and the fill valve refills it over and over.

Fill valve failing

The tank fills slowly, overfills or hisses constantly.

Handle or chain problem

The toilet will not flush properly or keeps running after a flush.

Supply line leak

Water drips from the connection behind the toilet.

Most tank repairs are quick and use inexpensive parts. A flapper that is warped or covered with mineral buildup can be replaced in minutes. If your toilet is older, we may suggest replacing the whole fill and flush assembly rather than chasing one worn part after another.

Leak at the base and wax ring replacement

A toilet sits on a flange attached to the floor drain, sealed by a wax ring. When the seal fails, you may see water at the base after a flush, smell sewer gas or feel the toilet rock. A wax ring is simple to replace, but the toilet has to be pulled to do it, and the flange underneath has to be sound.

If the flange is cracked, corroded or sits below the finished floor, the new ring will not seal. We repair flanges with repair rings or new flanges and set the toilet level. A leaking base that has been ignored can rot the subfloor, so it is worth fixing early.

Clogged toilets and repeat blockages

A plunger clears most single clogs. If a toilet clogs repeatedly, the cause may be deeper than the bowl: a partial blockage in the branch, a slow main line or a vent problem. The drain cleaning page explains what we do when the clog is below the fixture, and a main line problem often shows up first as a toilet that gurgles.

Installing a new toilet

We install toilets that you buy or we supply, and we explain rough-in size, the distance from the wall to the center of the flange, before you shop, because a mismatch means a return trip. A new toilet is set on a new wax ring or seal, connected to a new supply line and tested before we leave. The plumbing installation page lists other fixture work we handle.

Toilets in older Upper Darby homes

Bathrooms in older homes often have cast iron closet bends and small, tight spaces. Removing and resetting a toilet there can reveal a corroded flange or a floor that has softened from years of slow leaks. We tell you what we see before we continue, and we do not keep going on a floor that has rotted until you have chosen how to proceed.
