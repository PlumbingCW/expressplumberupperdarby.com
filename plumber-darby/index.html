Plumbing in a borough settled in 1682

Darby was settled in 1682 and incorporated as a borough on May 3, 1853. It covers about 0.84 square miles and has roughly 10,715 residents and 3,916 housing units. Landmarks include the Darby Friends Meeting House from 1805, the Friends Burial Ground from 1682 and the Darby Free Library, which dates to 1743.

A town this old holds housing from many eras, and a plumber has to expect surprises. We ask about the age of the house and the pipe on every call, and we explain what we find before we open a wall.

What fails first in an old house

Galvanized supply

Low pressure and rusty water point to pipe that has narrowed inside.

Cast iron drains

Rust and cracks cause leaks and slow drainage. See repiping for supply replacement.

Lead and brass

Old fittings can fail. We replace what we find, and we tell you what we see.

Basement fixtures

Floor drains and laundry tubs sit at the lowest point and collect backups.

Emergency calls any time of day

Our emergency line is open around the clock for Darby. A pipe that bursts on a holiday gets the same attention as a weekday call. Before we arrive

  • Find the main shutoffIt is usually where the water line enters the house near the meter. Turn it clockwise to stop a leak.
