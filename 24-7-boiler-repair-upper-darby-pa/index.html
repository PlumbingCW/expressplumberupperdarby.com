24/7 Boiler Repair for Upper Darby, PA Homes

A boiler that fails in January turns into an emergency quickly, and many Upper Darby, PA homes are heated by one.

This guide explains what to check yourself, what not to touch and when to call for 24/7 help.

Safety first

If you smell gas, which is often described as rotten eggs, leave the house, call the gas utility or 911 from outside and do not use switches, lighters or phones inside. We do not promise response times for gas emergencies, because the utility has to make the house safe first.

Carbon monoxide is another danger with fuel-burning heat. Working alarms on every floor are important. If one sounds, get everyone outside and call 911.

Simple things to check

  1. Check that the thermostat is set to heat and above the room temperature, and that its batteries are good if it uses them.
  2. Look at the boiler for a switch or power cord and make sure the power is on.
  3. Check the pressure gauge. Most home boilers run in a modest range when cold, and the manual gives the number.
  4. Look for a tripped breaker or a blown fuse.
  5. Check whether the gas shutoff to the boiler is open.

If those look normal and the boiler still will not run, press the reset once according to the manual. If it locks out again, do not keep resetting it, because repeated resets can flood the burner with fuel.

A leaking boiler can lose pressure quickly and shut down. If water is visible, turn off the boiler and close the water feed valve if you know where it is. Our boiler repair page lists what we check.

What a 24/7 call looks like

When you call the line at night, we ask whether you smell gas, whether the boiler has power, what the gauge reads and whether any water is leaking. We then book the first available plumber. Same-day service is available for calls that can safely wait for daylight, and we tell you how to keep the house safe while you wait.

Heating work can involve a heating contractor or the gas utility as well. We tell you plainly when part of the job belongs to someone else.

What to tell us on the call

  • Whether you smell gas or have a carbon monoxide alarm sounding.
  • What the boiler does when you try to start it, and any codes on the display.
  • The pressure on the gauge and whether you see water anywhere.
  • The age and type of the boiler if you know them.
  • Whether anyone in the house is elderly, very young or unwell.

Those details help us decide how urgent the call is and what to bring. A house with an infant and no heat in a cold snap is handled differently from a mild evening with a noisy boiler. Being clear and calm on the phone gets you help faster than a long story.

Gas, permits and winter in Upper Darby

Upper Darby Township has adopted the Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code and requires permits for residential alterations and repairs. Permit rules vary by job, so ask the township Department of Licenses and Inspection what applies to yours.

Upper Darby Township Code Chapter 265 says plumbing contractors working in the township must employ a licensed master plumber, and only the master plumber can apply for plumbing permits. Plumbing licenses in the township expire on March 31 each year.

Heating work that involves gas piping or replacing a boiler can require permits and inspection, so ask the Department of Licenses and Inspection what applies before work begins. The gas utility handles gas leaks and meter issues.

Prepare before the cold arrives

  • Have the boiler serviced in early fall, before the first cold snap.
  • Know where the gas shutoff, the power switch and the water feed valve are.
  • Bleed radiators that stay cold at the top.
  • Keep carbon monoxide alarms current and tested.
  • Keep a space heater rated for indoor use only as a short backup, never as a long-term heat source.

A boiler is connected to your water system, so a leak can affect your hot water too. The water heater repair page covers related problems when hot water comes from an indirect tank.

Repair or replace

Parts for older boilers can be hard to find, and an old heat exchanger that has failed is rarely worth repairing. We give you the repair price and, where it makes sense, the replacement price so you can compare. We do not push replacement when a repair is sound.

We serve Lansdowne and Haverford Township close to Upper Darby, and you can read about them on our Lansdowne plumber and Haverford Township plumber pages.

Keep warm while you wait

Close off unused rooms, open interior doors to share heat in the rooms you use, dress in layers and keep faucets dripping slightly on exterior walls if a hard freeze is forecast. Those steps slow cooling and reduce the risk of frozen pipes while the boiler is repaired.
