What Is a Sewer Lateral Inspection? What Upper Darby, PA Homes Need to Know

A sewer lateral is the pipe that carries waste from your house to the public sewer main, and an inspection puts a small camera inside it to show what condition it is in.

Many people first hear the term when they are buying a house, having a backup or planning a repair.

What the lateral is

Inside your house, drains join a main line that leaves the building. Outside, that line continues as the lateral until it connects to the sewer main under the street or in an easement. Who owns which part depends on the municipality and the sewer authority, so you should confirm it for your address.

Because the lateral is buried, problems stay hidden until water backs up. Roots, cracked pipe, sags and grease buildup all develop slowly.

How the inspection works

  1. 1

    Access

    The technician finds a cleanout or removes a toilet to reach the line.

  2. 2

    Camera run

    A flexible rod with a camera and light is pushed through the pipe.

  3. 3

    Locate

    A transmitter on the camera lets the crew mark where a problem sits and how deep.

  4. 4

    Review

    You see the video, and the technician explains what is normal and what is not.

The camera records the pipe material, the joints, any roots, cracks, offsets and sags, and the connection to the main where it can be seen. Our sewer inspection page describes how we report findings.

What an inspection can find

What you seeWhat it meansPossible next step
Roots at jointsWater is leaking in, roots followCutting or jetting, possibly lining
Cracks or breaksPipe has failed at a pointSpot repair, lining or replacement
Belly or sagWaste settles and collectsMonitor, jet or replace the section
Grease and scaleBuildup narrows the pipeCleaning, see sewer line cleaning
Offset jointPipe sections have shiftedRepair depending on severity

An inspection does not fix anything by itself. It tells you whether you need cleaning, repair or nothing at all. If the pipe is in good shape, you have a baseline for the future.

When to schedule one

  • You are buying a home and the sewer line is old or unknown.
  • You have had repeated slow drains or backups.
  • You see wet or green spots in the yard along the line.
  • You plan a major renovation or want a baseline before one.

After a backup, cleaning is the first step, and the sewer line cleaning page covers that. If the camera shows damage, the sewer line repair page covers the options.

Reading the video with your plumber

The video is more useful when someone walks you through it. Ask the technician to pause on anything unusual and say what it is. A line of shiny wet pipe is normal. A fuzzy white mass at a joint is usually root hair. A dark band across the bottom may be a sag with standing water.

Keep the recording. It documents the condition on the day it was made, so you can compare it later, share it with a second contractor or give it to a buyer. A written summary with distances from the cleanout helps anyone who comes later.

An inspection also tells you what is not wrong. Sometimes a repeating backup traces to a clog in a fixture or a partially closed valve and not the lateral at all. Knowing that saves you from paying for work you do not need.

Sewer lines and rules in Upper Darby

Ask Upper Darby Township which portion of the lateral you are responsible for and who to call for the public main. We do not assume the answer, because it depends on the property and the sewer authority.

Upper Darby Township has adopted the Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code and requires permits for residential alterations and repairs. Permit rules vary by job, so ask the township Department of Licenses and Inspection what applies to yours.

Upper Darby Township Code Chapter 265 says plumbing contractors working in the township must employ a licensed master plumber, and only the master plumber can apply for plumbing permits. Plumbing licenses in the township expire on March 31 each year.

Older housing and what we see

Houses in the older boroughs near Upper Darby, such as Darby and Lansdowne, were built across many decades, so laterals vary in age and material. Cast iron, clay and plastic all show up. We do not assume any material, and the video tells us which one we have.

Questions to ask your inspector

  1. Will you give me the video and a written summary?
  2. How do you handle a line with no cleanout?
  3. Will you locate the depth of any problem?
  4. What are my options if you find damage?
