Installing a sink drain and supply lines

Installing a sink drain sounds simple, and it is when the trap and the wall stub line up. In older homes they often do not. The wall drain may be at a different height, the pipe may be galvanized and corroded or the trap may be in the wrong place. We adapt the connection, replace corroded sections and make sure the drain slopes correctly.

On supply lines, we install new shutoff valves and braided lines so a future leak can be stopped with a quarter turn. We test every connection under pressure before we leave.

PEX and other modern materials

PEX tubing is a flexible plastic pipe used for supply lines. It resists corrosion and is quicker to run through finished spaces than rigid pipe. Not every job needs it, and in some places copper is the better choice. We explain what we use and why. Installing PEX plumbing correctly depends on the right fittings and tools, which is why we do the connections ourselves rather than leave them half done.

Water softeners and filtration

Water softeners and whole-house filters are popular installs, but the right choice depends on the water. We do not state a hardness number for Upper Darby, because it varies by system and by year. Test your tap with a hardness strip, or ask your water utility for its annual water quality report, and bring the result to the call.

A softener needs a drain, a power outlet, a bypass valve and a place for salt. A filter needs a clear idea of what you want to remove, since taste, chlorine and sediment are different problems. We install the unit, set the bypass and explain the maintenance. We do not promise results that depend on water we have not tested.

Backflow devices and gas appliance connections

Some installs involve backflow prevention, such as a device on an irrigation line, and some involve gas connections for water heaters and appliances. Requirements vary by property and by the township, so we explain what applies and what the township decides. Gas work always puts safety first. If you smell gas, leave and call the gas utility or 911 before you call us.

Installing next to what already exists

New work connects to old plumbing, and old plumbing is the unknown. A garbage disposal, a toilet or a faucet may need valves that have never been turned. See toilet repair and installation, faucet repair and garbage disposal installation for the details on each.

Choosing between doing it now and doing it later

Planned work is cheaper than emergency work. Replacing old shutoff valves, swapping brittle supply lines and updating a failing fixture before it leaks costs a fraction of what a flooded floor does. If you are remodeling a kitchen or bath, that is the best time to update the plumbing behind the walls, because the access is already open.
