Jammed, humming or dead

A disposal that hums but does not spin is jammed. A disposal that does nothing may have tripped its reset button or lost power. A disposal that leaks has failed seals or a loose connection. Each one has a different fix.

We ask what you hear and see: humming, silence, a burning smell, water from the bottom or sides, or water backing up in the other sink basin. Those details sort most calls before we arrive.

Clearing a jam safely

  1. Switch off the power at the wall switch and, if you can, the breaker.
  2. Look for the hex socket in the bottom of the unit and turn it with the hex key that came with the disposal to free the flywheel.
  3. Press the red reset button on the bottom.
  4. Restore power and run cold water while you test it.

If it jams again right away, something is stuck, the motor is weak or the unit is worn out. Do not keep resetting it, because that can burn out the motor.

Replacing and installing a disposal

Homeowners ask whether installing a garbage disposal is easy. It can be, when a disposal is replacing another and the mounting and wiring match. It gets harder with a new installation: a switch and power may be needed, the drain plumbing may need to be reworked, and the dishwasher connection has to be done right.

We install disposals, reconnect the dishwasher drain and the supply line and test the whole system. We also replace a disposal when the old one is too weak for the household. A larger motor handles more food waste and jams less.

Disposal and drain problems together

A kitchen sink that backs up is not always the disposal. Grease buildup in the line below can fail to clear even when the disposal runs. The drain cleaning page explains the line, and the hydro jetting page covers heavy grease. If the sink is leaking at the faucet, see faucet repair.

What should not go into a disposal

  • Grease and oil, which coat the line and clog it.
  • Coffee grounds in large amounts, which pack into a sludge.
  • Fibrous items such as celery stalks and corn husks.
  • Bones, fruit pits and hard shells.
  • Pasta, rice and potato peels, which swell and gum up.

Choosing a replacement disposal

Horsepower, feed type and build quality separate a disposal that lasts from one that jams every month. A quarter or half horsepower unit suits a light household, while a larger motor handles more food and quieter use. Stainless grinding parts resist wear longer than plated ones, and sound insulation helps in an open kitchen. We recommend a unit that fits how your household cooks and not the biggest one in the box.
