Emergency plumber

When you need an emergency plumber in Upper Darby

An emergency plumber is the right call when water is moving where it should not, when sewage is backing up, when there is no heat or hot water in winter or when a fixture will not shut off. Our emergency line is open 24/7, and a same-day appointment is available for problems that are urgent but not flooding.

When you call, we ask three things: where the water is, whether you can shut it off and what is at risk. Those answers decide how we prioritize the visit, and we stay on the phone to help with the shutoff. We do not promise exact arrival times, because they depend on traffic and on other calls.

  • A burst or frozen pipe: see our burst pipe repair page for shutoff steps.
  • A clogged or backed-up drain: our drain cleaning team uses cables and jetters.
  • No hot water or a leaking tank: water heater repair comes first, replacement if it is time.
  • Sewage in the basement: sewer line repair and a camera inspection.
  • A hidden leak or a spinning meter: leak detection finds it before a wall is opened.

Local knowledge

Plumbing across Upper Darby neighborhoods

Upper Darby Township has about 85,681 residents on 7.83 square miles, and its housing ranges from early twentieth-century twins to later rowhouses and apartment buildings. Drexel Hill is the largest residential community, with ZIP code 19026, and most of the rest of the township uses 19082. Cobbs Creek runs along the township's eastern border with Philadelphia.

Around the 69th Street Transportation Center in Terminal Square, buildings mix shops, offices and apartments, so a leak in one unit often reaches another. Our commercial plumbing team handles floor drains, water heaters and leaks in those buildings, including after hours.

Residential streets in Drexel Hill, Primos, Secane, Beverly Hills, Highland Park, Bywood, Stonehurst, Aronimink, Kirklyn and Westbrook Park have houses of many ages. Older ones often have older supply pipe and cast iron drains. When we visit, we ask the age of the house first, because it shapes the likely cause. The repiping page explains when old pipe is worth replacing.

Along Garrett Road and State Road, small businesses depend on working drains and hot water. A restaurant or shop that loses either one cannot open, so we treat those calls as urgent. Near the Tower Theater at 69th and Ludlow Streets, older commercial buildings share long drain runs, and a camera inspection often finds the problem faster than guessing.
