Gas safety comes first

If you smell gas, which is often described as rotten eggs, do not stay to troubleshoot. Leave the building, call the gas utility or 911 from outside and wait until the utility says it is safe. We do not give response times, because safety work by the utility takes priority. After the utility clears the house, we are available to diagnose the boiler.

Carbon monoxide is also a risk with fuel-burning heating. Working carbon monoxide alarms on each floor are important. If an alarm sounds, get outside and call 911.

No heat, noises and leaks

No heat or no hot water

The boiler may have lost ignition, tripped a safety, run low on pressure or failed a pump or valve.

Banging or kettling

Mineral or sludge buildup and trapped air can make a boiler noisy.

Leaks

Water at the base can come from a relief valve, a fitting, the expansion tank or a failing boiler.

Pressure problems

Pressure that is too low or too high points to a leak, a bad expansion tank or a faulty fill valve.

Many homes in the area are heated by hot water radiators or baseboard served by a boiler, and the older systems can run for decades. Age brings wear, and a boiler that fails in a cold snap is a common reason for a call.

Repair or replace

Parts for older boilers can be hard to find. When repair costs approach the price of a new unit, or when the heat exchanger has failed, replacement may be the better option. We explain the repair first and give both numbers when replacement is in play. Heating contractors may handle some of the larger boiler replacement and gas piping jobs, and we tell you when a job is outside our scope.

Boilers and the rest of the plumbing

A boiler is connected to your water supply and often feeds the domestic hot water. A leak at the boiler may involve a supply valve, and a failing indirect water heater is similar to the water heater repair problems we handle. If the leak is bigger, see burst pipe repair for shutoff steps.

Keeping a boiler running through winter

  • Know where the boiler shutoff and the water feed valve are.
  • Check the pressure gauge occasionally and note what normal looks like.
  • Bleed radiators that stay cold at the top.
  • Have the system serviced before the heating season, not during a cold snap.

Hot water heating in older homes

Many older houses in and around Upper Darby are heated by a boiler that sends hot water to radiators or baseboards. Those systems are durable, but they depend on pressure, circulation and a working burner. When one part fails, the whole house goes cold, which is why a winter no-heat call is among the most urgent we take.

We check the simple things first, such as the thermostat, the power and the gas supply, then move to the pressure, the circulator and the controls. We tell you what we find and what it costs before replacing parts, and we explain when a heating contractor is the better call for a larger boiler project.
