Natural Solutions for Clogged Drains in Upper Darby, PA Homes

Many slow drains clear with household items and a little patience, and natural methods are gentler on pipes than harsh chemicals.

They also have limits, and this guide explains where they help and where a plumber is the better call.

Start with the plunger and the trap

A plunger is the best first tool for a sink, tub or toilet. Seal it over the opening, add enough water to cover the cup and use steady up and down strokes. For a double sink, block the other drain first. If the water drains and the clog is a hair ball in a shower or tub, pull the strainer and remove what you see.

Under a sink, the curved trap collects debris by design. Place a bucket under it, unscrew the slip nuts and clear the trap by hand. This often fixes a kitchen or bathroom sink that is slow, and it costs nothing.

Baking soda and vinegar

  1. Remove standing water from the sink if you can.
  2. Pour about half a cup of baking soda into the drain.
  3. Add a half cup of white vinegar and cover the drain for several minutes.
  4. Flush with hot tap water.

The fizz can loosen light buildup and freshen a drain, but it does not clear a solid blockage. It works on thin films of grease and soap, not on a ball of hair or a stuck object. Never mix it with a chemical drain cleaner.

Hot water and what to avoid

Hot tap water flushLight grease and soapNot boiling water on plastic pipes or porcelain

Hand snakeHair and debris within reachCan scratch fixtures, stop if it jams

Boiling water can soften plastic joints and crack a cold porcelain bowl, so use hot water from the tap. Do not pour grease down the drain and then chase it with hot water. It will cool and settle farther down the line.

Prevent the next clog

  • Use a hair catcher in showers and tubs.
  • Scrape plates into the trash, not the sink.
  • Collect cooking grease in a can and throw it away.
  • Run hot water for a few seconds after using the kitchen sink.
  • Flush only toilet paper, and put wipes in the trash even when they say flushable.

A kitchen drain with a disposal needs its own care, and the garbage disposal repair page lists what should never go into one.

Using a hand snake without damage

A small hand snake, sometimes called a drain auger, can pull hair out of a tub or sink. Feed the cable into the drain until you feel resistance, then crank the handle while pushing gently. When it catches the clog, pull the cable out slowly and wipe off what comes with it. Do not force it if it jams, because you can scratch a fixture or kink the cable.

Snakes work on clogs within a few feet of the opening. A longer or deeper blockage needs a professional machine, and a toilet needs a special closet auger that protects the porcelain. If the cable will not go in at all, the drain may be crushed or blocked by something solid.

Enzyme cleaners are the other gentle option. They use bacteria to digest organic buildup slowly, and they work best as regular maintenance rather than as an emergency fix. Give them a few days and follow the label.

Local plumbing rules and resources

Upper Darby Township Code Chapter 265 says plumbing contractors working in the township must employ a licensed master plumber, and only the master plumber can apply for plumbing permits. Plumbing licenses in the township expire on March 31 each year.

Contractors who do at least $5,000 a year in home improvement must register with the Pennsylvania Attorney General, and you can look one up at hicsearch.attorneygeneral.gov.

Ask Upper Darby Township how to handle household grease and wipes in your area, since public sewer systems are especially affected by what goes down the drain.

When natural methods are not enough

If several drains are slow at once, if water backs up in a basement floor drain when you run the washing machine or if a clog returns within days, the problem is likely in the main drain line. At that point you need a machine and a camera, not a home remedy. See drain cleaning for how we approach it.

A plumber can also check whether roots, a sag or a cracked pipe is behind a repeating clog, and the sewer inspection page explains how the camera helps.

A simple plan

  1. Plunge, then clear the trap and strainer.
  2. Try a hot water flush and, if you like, baking soda and vinegar.
  3. Use a hand snake carefully for hair clogs.
  4. If more than one drain is slow or the clog returns, call a plumber.

Sources: the International Plumbing Code (IPC) sets drain and trap standards, and plastic pipe makers publish temperature guidelines you should check before using hot water.
