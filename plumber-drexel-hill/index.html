What Drexel Hill is and where it sits

Drexel Hill is a census-designated place in the western part of Upper Darby Township, about eight miles from Center City Philadelphia. It covers about 3.2 square miles. At the 2020 census it had 29,181 residents and 12,384 housing units, which means it holds more than a third of the township's population.

It borders Haverford Township to the north and the Kirklyn, Highland Park, Beverly Hills and Bywood areas to the east. Lansdowne lies to the southeast, and Clifton Heights and Westbrook Park lie to the south. Darby Creek forms its southwestern and southern edge. Township Line Road, State Road, Burmont Road, Garrett Road, Lansdowne Avenue and Marshall Road run through or beside it.

A big community with many kinds of buildings

The Census Bureau counts housing units but does not say here how old they are or what type they are, so we do not guess. What the record does show is variety. Drexel Hill includes the Drexelbrook apartment complex, older estates such as Thornfield on Garrett Road and a long list of residential streets. The Media and Sharon Hill trolley lines, routes 101 and 102, start at the 69th Street Transportation Center and serve the area.

That mix matters for plumbing. A detached house usually has its own shutoff and water heater. An apartment or condo may share risers and drains with neighbors, so a leak can reach a unit below. We ask the building type first and plan the visit around it.

Plumbing services people in Drexel Hill ask about

  • A slow or backed-up sink, tub or floor drain: see drain cleaning.
  • No hot water or a leaking tank: start with water heater repair or replacement.
  • A wet ceiling, a high water bill or a running meter: leak detection traces the source.
  • Sewage smells or repeated backups: a sewer camera inspection shows what is in the line.
  • A pipe that has split: burst pipe repair covers shutoff steps.

Before the plumber arrives

  • Find the main shutoffIt is usually where the water line enters the house near the meter. Turn it clockwise to stop a leak.
  • Open the lowest faucetThis drains the lines and lowers pressure while you wait.
  • Stay clear of wet electricsKeep away from outlets and the panel if the floor is wet.
  • Protect what you canLift rugs and boxes off the floor and take photos of any damage.
