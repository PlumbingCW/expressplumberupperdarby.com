When a backup calls for jetting instead of a cable

A cable punches through a clog. A hydro jetter does something different: it sends water at high pressure through a hose with a nozzle that fires jets backward, pulling the hose forward while the jets cut the buildup off the pipe wall and flush it downstream.

Reach for jetting when a cable got stuck partway down the line, when the clog returns within weeks, when grease has coated the pipe, or when fine roots hang on the pipe walls. A line that backs up in more than one fixture at once is a main line problem, and jetting is often the fastest way to restore flow without opening the ground.

What jetting removes and what it cannot fix

  • Grease and soap scum that coat kitchen and laundry lines.
  • Mineral scale and scale flakes inside older cast iron.
  • Fine and moderate root growth entering at joints.
  • Silt, sand and debris settled in low spots.

Jetting does not fix a broken pipe. If the pipe is cracked, collapsed or offset at a joint, clearing it only buys time. A camera look before and after tells you which situation you have, and if the pipe is damaged we move to sewer line repair options and explain them in plain terms.

Why jetting is not right for every pipe

High-pressure water on a fragile or failing pipe can make a bad situation worse. Old clay tile with open joints, pipes with an obvious collapse, and thin-walled pipe in poor condition can be damaged by pressure. That is why we look first when we can, adjust the pressure and nozzle to the pipe, and sometimes recommend a lighter approach.

This is also why we do not sell jetting as a miracle step. When the camera shows a pipe near the end of its life, we say so, and the better money is often in a repair or lining rather than a repeat jetting visit.

Kitchen lines and restaurant grease

Restaurants and shops around Terminal Square and along State Road send a lot of grease down their drains. Grease sticks to the pipe wall and hardens, and cables leave it behind. Jetting is the standard answer for a heavy grease line, followed by a maintenance schedule so it does not rebuild. The commercial plumbing page covers that side of the work.

In a house, a kitchen line that clogs more than twice a year is worth jetting once. The drain cleaning page explains the cable steps that come first for lighter clogs.

Before and after a jetting visit

Before jetting, we run a camera or check the cleanout to confirm the pipe can handle pressure. We tell you if it cannot. After jetting, we run the camera again so you can see the clean pipe and any damage that was hidden under the buildup. That second look matters, because jetting can reveal cracks or offsets that a blockage was hiding.

A jetted line is clean, but it is not new. If the pipe has defects, the clog will come back at the same spot. We tell you so, and we explain whether a repair or a cleaning schedule makes more sense for your pipe. For lines in lighter trouble, drain cleaning with a cable may be enough.

Safety before and after jetting

Keep people and pets out of the room around a cleanout while the jetter runs.

  • Close lids on nearby fixtures so spray cannot escape.
  • Do not use drain chemicals before a visit, and tell us if you did.
  • Wear gloves and wash hands after any contact with drain water.
  • Open windows if a sewer smell builds up.
