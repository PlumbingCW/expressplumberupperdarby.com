Where we work in Clifton Heights

Clifton Heights covers about 0.63 square miles, with Darby Creek along its north and northeast edge. The borough borders Lansdowne, Aldan and Upper Darby. Its population peaked at 10,268 in 1960 and is about 6,863 today, so the housing is dense and mostly older.

Kent Mills, a carpet-yarn mill that began in 1839, shaped the streets. Many of the older homes are the small rowhouses and twins built for millworkers. Those houses have narrow basements and tight pipe runs, which affects how we work.

Tin Kettle Hill

Homes on and around Oak Avenue sit on sloped ground, so drain lines can run at steeper angles than in flat areas.

Compact houses with shared walls often have older cast iron drains.

Near Darby Creek

Homes close to the creek can have basement moisture, so we check whether it is a leak or groundwater.

Sewer and drain problems in older rows

In a row of attached houses, a main drain can serve several homes, and a clog in one place backs up into another. If your basement drain gurgles or sewage appears, stop running water, then call. Our sewer line repair and sewer line cleaning pages explain how we clear and fix main lines.

Before any digging, we look inside with a camera, as described on the sewer inspection page. A recording shows whether the problem is roots, a collapse or a grease buildup, and it lets you see it too.
