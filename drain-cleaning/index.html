When a clogged drain is an emergency

One slow bathroom sink can wait until morning. Water coming up in a tub when the washing machine drains, a toilet that bubbles when you run the kitchen tap, or sewage in a basement floor drain cannot. Those signs point to the main line, which every fixture in the house shares.

If more than one fixture is affected, treat it as a main line problem and call right away. We take emergency drain calls at night and on weekends across Upper Darby Township, and same-day appointments are available for the less urgent ones.

How we find where the clog is

The first question is whether the blockage is in a single fixture drain or in the line that serves the whole house. A bathroom sink that drains slowly while the shower runs fine is a branch problem. A tub and a basement floor drain that both back up are a main line problem.

For branch lines we use a hand or drum cable with the right cutting head for the pipe size. For main lines we run a larger machine, and when a clog keeps returning we put a camera in the line to see roots, a belly in the pipe or a cracked joint. If the camera shows damage, the fix moves to sewer line repair and we tell you that before any digging is discussed.

Kitchen sink clogs and grease

Kitchen drains clog differently from bathroom drains. Cooking grease cools inside the pipe and holds food particles against it, so the line narrows a little with every dish load until it stops. A cable opens a hole through that buildup, but the grease on the pipe walls stays behind.

When a kitchen line clogs more than twice a year, the walls are coated. We then clear it with a cutting head sized to scrape the pipe, and for heavy buildup we recommend hydro jetting because water at pressure removes the coating a cable only pierces. If a disposal sits on that sink, we check whether it is part of the problem, and you can read more about that on the garbage disposal repair page.

Slow drains, snaking and what actually helps

Homeowners often ask whether pouring acid or a store-bought clog remover through a drain is a good first move. Strong chemical openers can sit in a fully blocked pipe, damage older metal and plastic joints, and leave caustic water for the plumber to work through. We would rather you skip them and call.

Natural options like hot water and a plunger work on a partial clog in a fixture trap. They do not reach a clog twenty feet down a branch line. If a plunger does not move it in a few firm tries, a cable is the right tool and will not hurt the pipe.

Repeat clogs in older Upper Darby homes

Pipes in homes from before 1960 are often cast iron or galvanized steel inside and clay tile outside. Cast iron develops rough scale on the inside wall that catches paper and grease. Clay tile joints let tree roots in, which is why a clog near the front yard keeps coming back after a cable clears it.

Anywhere in the township, a mature street tree close to the lateral is a common root source. If the camera shows roots, jetting can clear the line now, but a recurring root clog is a repair question. We lay out the options, from a spot repair to pipe lining, in plain terms before you decide anything.

Floor drains, laundry lines and basement backups

A basement floor drain that backs up when the washer runs is usually telling you the main line is partly blocked. The drain sits at the lowest point of the house, so it is the first place water shows. We check the cleanout, clear the line and ask whether the other fixtures were slow beforehand, because that points to how far down the problem sits.

If sewage comes up and the cause is a main blockage, see sewer line cleaning. Do not run water until the line is cleared, because everything you flush goes to the lowest opening in the house.
