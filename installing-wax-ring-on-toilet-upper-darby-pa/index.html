Installing a Wax Ring on a Toilet in Upper Darby, PA

The wax ring is the seal between a toilet and the drain, and when it fails, water or sewer gas escapes at the base.

This guide explains how to tell if your ring has failed, how it is replaced and why the flange underneath matters more than most people expect.

How to tell the wax ring has failed

  • Water pools around the base of the toilet, especially after flushing.
  • The toilet rocks when you sit on it.
  • A sewer smell comes from the bathroom even when the trap is full.
  • The floor near the toilet feels soft or looks stained.

Do not confuse this with a leak from the tank or the supply line. Dry the toilet, put a few sheets of toilet paper around the base and flush. Wet paper at the base points to the ring. Wet paper only at the tank or the supply connection points elsewhere.

What you need

You need a new wax ring or a wax-free seal, new closet bolts, a putty knife, rags, a bucket and a helper, because a toilet is heavy and awkward. Wear gloves. A flange repair ring is worth having on hand if the old flange looks damaged.

Step by step

  1. Shut off the water at the valve behind the toilet, flush and hold the handle to drain the tank. Sponge out what remains.
  2. Disconnect the supply line and remove the nuts on the closet bolts.
  3. Rock the toilet gently to break the seal and lift it straight up. Set it on towels, and stuff a rag into the drain opening to block sewer gas.
  4. Scrape off the old wax and inspect the flange for cracks, rust and height.
  5. Place the new ring and set the toilet straight down on the bolts. Press down evenly and do not twist it.
  6. Tighten the nuts a little at a time, reconnect the supply line, restore water and check for leaks.

Do not over-tighten the nuts, because porcelain cracks. Run several flushes and watch the base.

The flange matters

The flange is the ring attached to the floor that the toilet bolts to. A new wax ring cannot seal against a flange that is cracked, corroded or sitting below the finished floor. Repair rings, extender kits and new flanges fix those problems, but the work gets more involved when the flange is rusted cast iron or the subfloor has rotted.

Mistakes that cause a second leak

The most common reason a new ring leaks is that the toilet was twisted or lifted after it was set. Once the wax touches the horn of the toilet and the flange, it should not be moved. If you need to adjust, lift it straight up and use a fresh ring.

The second most common reason is a toilet that is not level or a floor that is not flat. A rocking toilet works the seal loose. Plastic shims under the base, trimmed flush after setting, keep it steady. Caulk around the base after it is set, but leave a small gap at the back in some setups so a future leak is visible rather than hidden.

  • Set the toilet straight down, never at an angle.
  • Use closet bolts that sit upright and are the right length.
  • Tighten the nuts evenly on both sides in small turns.
  • Wait to caulk until you have flushed and checked for leaks.

Permits and licensing in Upper Darby

Upper Darby Township has adopted the Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code and requires permits for some residential alterations and repairs. A simple wax ring swap on an existing toilet is routine, but moving a toilet or changing drain pipe is different. Permit rules vary, so ask the Department of Licenses and Inspection before you start.

Upper Darby Township Code Chapter 265 says plumbing contractors working in the township must employ a licensed master plumber, and only the master plumber can apply for plumbing permits. Plumbing licenses in the township expire on March 31 each year.

When to call a plumber instead

Call a plumber if the flange is damaged, if the floor is soft, if water has been leaking for a while or if you cannot lift the toilet safely. A plumber also checks the drain for a partial clog, because a toilet that clogs often can strain the seal.

Wax ring or wax-free seal

Wax-free seals are rubber or foam and are reusable. They are forgiving if the toilet needs to be lifted again. A wax ring is simple and cheap, and it works well when the flange is sound and the toilet sits flat. Either is fine. The flange condition matters more than the seal type.

Sources: according to the International Plumbing Code (IPC), a toilet must be set on a sound, sealed flange, and the seal maker's guidelines on the package cover the rest.
