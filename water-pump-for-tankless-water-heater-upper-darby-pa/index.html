Water Pump for a Tankless Water Heater in Upper Darby, PA

People search for a water pump for a tankless heater for two different reasons: they want hot water faster at the faucet, or they want to flush mineral scale out of the unit.

Those are two separate pumps, and this guide explains each so you buy the right one.

Two pumps, two jobs

PumpWhat it doesWhen you need it
Recirculation pumpMoves hot water through the loop so it is ready at the tapLong waits for hot water
Descaling flush pumpCirculates a cleaning solution through the heat exchangerAnnual or periodic maintenance
Booster pumpRaises water pressureLow flow rate through the heater

A tankless heater does not store hot water, so it makes it on demand. That is efficient, but there is a delay while the water travels from the heater to the faucet. That delay is what a recirculation pump addresses.

Recirculation pumps

A recirculation system keeps a small flow moving between the heater and the fixtures, so hot water is near the tap. Some heaters have a built-in pump or a buffer tank. Others use an add-on pump with a return line or a crossover valve under the farthest sink.

  • Dedicated return line systems are the most efficient, but need a pipe back to the heater.
  • Crossover valve systems use the cold line as the return, and they warm that line a little.
  • Timers and temperature controls reduce running time and wasted energy.

A recirculation pump costs money to run and loses some heat through the pipes. It is worth it when waiting for hot water is a daily annoyance, less so if it is a small house with the heater near the taps.

The flush pump and descaling

Minerals in water leave scale inside the heat exchanger of a tankless heater, and scale lowers flow and efficiency. Flushing circulates a mild acid solution, often food-grade vinegar or a descaling product, through the unit using a small pump and two hoses connected to isolation valves.

  1. Turn off the gas or power to the heater and close the water supply valves.
  2. Connect the pump and hoses to the service ports on the isolation valves.
  3. Run the solution through the unit for the time the manufacturer recommends.
  4. Flush with clean water, remove the hoses, open the valves and restore power.

Follow your heater's manual. Some manufacturers set the flush interval based on water hardness. Our tankless water heater page covers flushing and repair, and the water heater repair page covers error codes and failures.

Cost of running a recirculation pump

A pump uses a small amount of electricity, but the real cost is heat loss. Hot water circulating through pipes gives up warmth to the house, and the heater has to make it up. A timer or a temperature sensor reduces that waste. Running the pump only in the morning and evening is a common setup.

The benefit is less water down the drain while you wait for it to warm up. In a house with a long pipe run to a bathroom, that can mean minutes of running cold water each time. If you wait only a few seconds today, a pump will not change your life.

Ask your installer to explain the choices on the table: a dedicated return line, a crossover valve, a built-in pump or a buffer tank. Each one fits different houses, and the right one depends on how your pipes are laid out.

Common mistakes with tankless pumps

  • Buying a recirculation pump the heater manufacturer does not approve.
  • Running the pump around the clock with no timer.
  • Skipping the isolation valves that make flushing possible.
  • Using a harsh cleaner that the manual does not allow.

Most of these are avoidable by reading the manual and asking the installer which parts they are using. If you already have a heater with no service valves, adding them is a small job that makes every future flush faster and cleaner.

A pump that is installed but never maintained is also a problem. Check it once a year for noise, leaks and heat, and replace it if it runs hot or grinds.

Choosing and installing a pump

Match the pump to the heater. Some manufacturers warn that an unapproved add-on can void a warranty. Check the manual and ask before you buy. Installation involves pipe changes, power for the pump and sometimes a new valve, so the work overlaps with water heater installation.

Signs your tankless heater needs attention

  • Water that turns lukewarm or fluctuates in temperature.
  • Error codes on the display.
  • Noises like popping or rumbling from the unit.
  • Lower flow even when water pressure elsewhere is fine.

Sources: the heater manufacturer's guidelines decide which pumps are approved and how often to flush, and Aqua Pennsylvania publishes annual water quality reports.
