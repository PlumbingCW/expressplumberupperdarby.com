What a sewer lateral inspection is

A sewer lateral inspection is a camera run through the pipe that connects your house to the municipal sewer main. A technician feeds a waterproof camera on a flexible cable through a cleanout and watches a live feed of the pipe wall. The footage shows roots, cracks, offsets, bellies, scale and debris.

The result is a clear picture of the pipe, and a recording you can keep. We note the distance from the cleanout of anything we find, so a repair can be planned from the same footage.

When an inspection makes sense

Before buying a home

The lateral is out of sight and expensive to fix, so know its condition first.

After a backup

A camera shows why it happened and whether it will happen again.

Repeat slow drains

A pipe that keeps clogging may be damaged, not dirty.

Before a major project

New landscaping, an addition or a driveway job should avoid a failing line.

What the camera can and cannot show

A camera sees the inside surface of the pipe, but it cannot judge strength. A pipe with a hairline crack can look fine and still be weak, and a rough interior may look worse than it is. We explain what we saw in context, and we tell you honestly when the footage is inconclusive.

The camera also cannot pass through a full blockage, which is why a line is sometimes cleaned before inspection. If a section is badly bellied, standing water may hide the pipe wall behind it.

Buying an older Upper Darby home

If you are buying a twin, rowhouse or single in Upper Darby, Drexel Hill or Primos, ask for a sewer lateral inspection as part of due diligence. Older homes often have clay or cast iron laterals, and replacing one is a major expense. A report in hand lets you negotiate or budget before closing.

Responsibility for the lateral and local rules about it vary, so ask the township. We can explain what we usually see and what a good report includes. If the camera shows damage, the next page to read is sewer line repair.

Inspection after a cleaning

If a line has been cleared, the camera often follows. It confirms the pipe is clear and shows whether the cause is a one-time blockage or an ongoing defect. Sewer line cleaning is the step that comes first for a line that is not flowing.

What you receive after the inspection

  1. The video recording, so you can watch it yourself or share it.
  2. A written note of what the camera saw, with distances from the access point.
  3. A plain explanation of what needs attention now, what can wait and what is normal.
  4. Options and prices for any repair, in writing, before any work begins.

The camera does not diagnose by itself. A skilled operator reads it, and a second opinion from another company is easier when you hold the recording. We encourage it for large repairs. If the video shows damage, the sewer line repair page lays out the options, from cleaning and spot repair to lining and replacement.
